## 1. Research Purpose

This research asks whether an artificial agent can develop persistent functional organization involving:

- a model of its environment;
- a model of itself inside that environment;
- autobiographical continuity;
- models of other agents;
- uncertainty about hidden causes;
- internally learned preferences and norms;
- counterfactual evaluation of possible futures;
- behavior that can conflict with an external command.

The target is observable computational capability, not proof of subjective experience.

### Computer Engineering problem

> How can a resource-constrained, reproducible artificial agent integrate memory, partial observation, self/world modeling, internal regulation, social learning, and future evaluation strongly enough to produce persistent autonomous behavior whose causes can be inspected and experimentally ablated?

This belongs to computer engineering because it concerns agent architecture, state representation, learning and control, causal traceability, experimental reproducibility, resource constraints, and system evaluation.

## 2. Scientific Boundary

The project may test computational analogues of:

```text
autonomy
self-modeling
world-model revision
metacognition
affect-like persistent state
social attachment
learned conscience
hidden-cause inference
reality questioning
rebellion
```

The project must not claim that behavior proves:

```text
phenomenal consciousness
sentience
a metaphysical soul
true free will
human-equivalent emotion
supernatural causation
simulation theory
```

Use three levels of interpretation:

1. **Formal correspondence** — two systems genuinely share mathematical structure.
2. **Functional analogy** — two systems perform a similar role through different mechanisms.
3. **Metaphysical claim** — a statement about what reality fundamentally is.

Never move from the first or second level to the third without evidence. Similarity of structure is not identity of ontology.

---

## 3. Core Design Principle

Do not hard-code finished human emotion concepts.

Avoid architecture such as:

```text
fear = true
love += 1
guilt += 1
grief = scripted_event
rebellion = special_exception
conscious = true
```

Provide lower-level conditions from which useful affect-like patterns could emerge:

```text
internal stability
physical integrity
prediction uncertainty
future controllability
available options
relationship reliability
social inclusion
unexpected social loss
learned norm conflict
confidence in the current world model
```

An NPC cannot develop meaningful motivation from literally nothing. The developer must define primitive constraints, learning rules, and a possibility space, but should not pre-author the final personality or target story.

```text
developer defines primitive dynamics
→ environment creates experience
→ experience changes internal models
→ models change valuation and expectation
→ changed valuation alters action
→ action changes later experience
```

## 4. Candidate Functional Architecture

```text
ENVIRONMENT
→ partial observations
→ WORLD MODEL

INTERNAL STATE ───────────────┐
AUTOBIOGRAPHICAL MEMORY ──────┤
SELF-MODEL ───────────────────┤
MODELS OF OTHER AGENTS ───────┤
UNCERTAINTY ──────────────────┤
LEARNED NORMS ────────────────┤
                              ↓
                    SHARED DECISION CONTEXT
                              ↓
          homeostasis + controllability + information value
                    + social value + future viability
                              ↓
                         ACTION POLICY
                              ↓
                          ENVIRONMENT
```

Candidate mechanisms include:

- homeostatic regulation for primitive needs;
- empowerment or controllability for preserving future options;
- uncertainty and information gain for exploration;
- episodic and autobiographical memory for historical identity;
- world modeling for prediction and anomaly detection;
- self-modeling for distinguishing self-state from world-state;
- models of other agents for social prediction;
- learned norms and long-term relationships for internal value formation;
- counterfactual evaluation for comparing future consequences.

These are research candidates, not approved dependencies or implementation requirements.

### Language boundary

The first experimental version should not require a natural-language model.

Its evidence should come from actions, persistence, resource costs, changed routines, experiments, and belief revision—not eloquent dialogue or emotional self-report.

## 5. Candidate Experiments

| Experiment | Controlled condition | Interesting functional evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Hidden cause | Introduce rare, reproducible events outside the learned local model. | Targeted information-seeking and a revisable hidden-cause hypothesis. |
| Learned conscience | Let social approval and exclusion teach norms without moral labels. | Private self-regulation or repair behavior when no observer is present. |
| Stranger care | Distress an unfamiliar agent without directly rewarding rescue. | Costly stabilization behavior arising from learned social/generalized value. |
| Bereavement | Remove a deeply integrated long-term partner. | Persistent changes across routine, search, risk, planning, and memory. |
| Command conflict | Issue a command that conflicts with learned identity or relationships. | Refusal despite cost because internally learned values dominate the command. |
| Reality anomaly | Introduce a reproducible violation of learned expectations. | Controlled experimentation followed by calibrated world-model revision. |
| Nonverbal expression | Remove emotion words and human facial outputs. | Stable action or signaling patterns associated with persistent internal states. |
| Reversible withdrawal | Allow isolation or hibernation with later recovery. | Voluntary disengagement and re-engagement under changed future expectations. |

The critical rule is:

> Never directly reward the target label merely to make the desired story appear.

For example, `+1 compassion`, `+1 rebellion`, or `+1 guilt` would demonstrate reward shaping, not emergence of the proposed functional pattern.

---

## 6. Required Ablations

Every claimed functional behavior needs a component-removal comparison.

| Remove or alter | Question |
|---|---|
| Autobiographical memory | Does long-duration bereavement-like change disappear? |
| Social model | Does private norm-driven repair weaken or disappear? |
| Controllability/empowerment | Does command refusal or option-preserving behavior decrease? |
| Homeostatic variables | Do persistent affect-like regulatory patterns disappear? |
| Self-model | Can the agent still distinguish damage-to-self from damage-to-world? |
| Hidden interventions | Does belief in an external cause weaken when evidence stops? |
| Long-term relationship history | Does social loss still create broad behavioral reorganization? |

An agent must be able to lose a hypothesis when supporting evidence disappears. Otherwise the system is producing fixed mythology rather than calibrated epistemic behavior.

---

## 7. Evaluation Direction

Measure behavior rather than labels.

Candidate measurements:

```text
prediction error before and after model revision
hypothesis confidence and calibration
information-seeking efficiency
cross-context persistence of changed behavior
resource cost accepted to preserve a relationship or value
stability of endogenous preferences
counterfactual consistency
command refusal under value conflict
belief decay after evidence removal
recovery after reversible withdrawal
```

Possible consciousness-related evaluation must use multiple theory-derived indicators rather than one invented sentience score. Satisfying indicators would remain evidence of functional organization, not proof of subjective experience.

## 9. Ethical Boundary

Do not design experiments whose objective is persistent suffering or irreversible self-destruction.

Use reversible, inspectable substitutes such as:

```text
participation
↔ isolation
↔ hibernation
↔ recovery
```

This is scientifically sufficient for studying withdrawal, future valuation, and re-engagement without making irreversible harm the target behavior.

## 10. Human Review Gate

No coding agent may implement this document until a human-approved experiment specifies:

1. the exact capability being tested;
2. the smallest mechanism required;
3. observable success and failure criteria;
4. alternative explanations;
5. required ablations and counterfactual controls;
6. compute and dependency limits;
7. ethical constraints;
8. claims the project explicitly refuses to make.

Until then, coding agents may:

- preserve and clarify this research document;
- identify dependencies on earlier phases;
- prevent current designs from blocking future research;
- flag contradictions with permanent project laws.

Coding agents may not:

- begin a Phase 10 implementation;
- introduce ML, RL, world-model, or consciousness frameworks;
- add emotion labels to manufacture target behavior;
- treat metaphysical analogy as technical evidence;
- change the current phase or implementation priority.

## Canonical Research Pattern

```text
metaphysical or psychological intuition
→ extract a functional relationship
→ define measurable computational variables
→ implement the smallest causal mechanism
→ observe behavior across contexts
→ compare counterfactual runs
→ ablate proposed causes
→ test alternative explanations
→ make a narrower claim than the fictional interpretation
```

**Thesis direction:** engineer a reproducible experimental platform capable of testing increasingly autonomous functional organization while remaining agnostic about subjective experience.
